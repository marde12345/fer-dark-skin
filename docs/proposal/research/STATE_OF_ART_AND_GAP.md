## 1. Review Strategy

**Decision: targeted literature review, not a Systematic Literature Review (SLR).**

Justification, per the guideline (`docs/proposal/guideline/PROPOSAL_GUIDELINE_EXTRACTION.md` §5, citing BAB 6 §6.3, p.107): an SLR requires a documented, reproducible protocol — defined databases, search strings, inclusion/exclusion criteria, staged screening with recorded counts (deduplication → title → abstract → full-text), and a quality-assessment procedure (Table 6.3, p.109–110). This phase does not have access to a bibliographic database with structured export/deduplication tooling (only general web search was available), cannot report defensible per-stage screening counts, and is explicitly scoped as a **proposal-stage** gap investigation, not the thesis's final literature chapter. The guideline explicitly permits a targeted review for narrow domains "as long as rigor is preserved" (p.107) — this review preserves rigor by verifying every citation against its actual source page and explicitly distinguishing "found and read" from "found but not independently verified."

Per the guideline's own explicit warning (`PROPOSAL_GUIDELINE_EXTRACTION.md` §5, p.107): **PRISMA is a reporting guideline, not a substitute for full SLR methodology** — this review does not claim to be, and is not labeled as, a PRISMA-compliant SLR.

**Search concepts** (concept-blocks per §6.2, p.104–106):
- Concept block 1 (problem): facial expression recognition, emotion recognition, expression classification, Neutral misclassification, Angry misclassification.
- Concept block 2 (population/context): skin tone, race, demographic bias, dark skin, Fitzpatrick scale, dataset representation.
- Concept block 3 (artifact/technology): ArcFace, face recognition embeddings, additive angular margin loss, frozen embeddings, transfer learning, face representation.
- Concept block 4 (outcome): accuracy disparity, fairness, cross-dataset generalization, dataset bias.

**Search tool used**: `WebSearch` (general web search, not a single bibliographic database — no access to Scopus/WoS/IEEE Xplore/ACM DL search-and-export tooling in this environment). **Actual per-database search counts are not available and are not fabricated here.** Search queries actually issued are listed below for auditability, consistent with the guideline's documentation requirement (§6.2, p.106):

1. `ArcFace Additive Angular Margin Loss face recognition Deng 2019 CVPR`

3. `Gender Shades Buolamwini Gebru intersectional accuracy disparities commercial gender classification`
4. `racial bias facial expression recognition skin tone accuracy disparity study`
5. `neutral expression misclassified angry race bias facial expression recognition Black faces`

**Inclusion logic**: a source was included only if (a) it was directly retrievable via search and, where a specific claim was extracted, independently confirmed by fetching the source page directly (not taken from the search engine's own summary alone), and (b) it was directly relevant to at least one of the five literature categories in Section 2 of the task instructions (FER generally; face representation/ArcFace; skin-tone/demographic robustness; FER error analysis; dataset/benchmark limitations).

**Exclusion logic**: general-audience news articles, psychology-only human-perception studies not about automated/algorithmic FER systems (several appeared in search results on "racial bias in angry-face recognition" — these are about *human* perceptual bias, not FER *systems*, and are excluded from the SOTA table below, though noted once in Section 6 as a boundary case), and any source whose authorship/venue could not be independently confirmed.

**Relevance criteria**: must bear directly on the practical problem (Neutral↔Angry-type FER error patterns), the candidate face-representation approach (ArcFace/embedding-based FER), or the skin-tone/demographic dimension of FER performance — not general computer-vision or general fairness literature outside these three.

**Evidence extraction approach**: for each included source, title/authors/year/venue were extracted directly from the fetched page (not inferred), and the main finding/limitation relevant to this thesis was summarized in the source's own terms, then explicitly separated from any thesis-specific interpretation.

## 2. Literature by Category

### A. Facial Expression Recognition (general)

- **Li & Deng (2020), "Deep Facial Expression Recognition: A Survey," IEEE Transactions on Affective Computing.** Confirmed via direct fetch (arxiv/whdeng.cn hosted copy). A broad survey of deep-learning FER methods, datasets, and open challenges — establishes that deep FER is a mature, actively surveyed field with well-known benchmark datasets, but the survey itself is general-purpose and does not focus on skin-tone/demographic robustness or on face-recognition-embedding-based approaches specifically.
- **Mollahosseini, Hasani & Mahoor, "AffectNet: A Database for Facial Expression, Valence, and Arousal Computing in the Wild," IEEE Transactions on Affective Computing (2017/2019).** AffectNet is one of the largest in-the-wild FER benchmark datasets (~450,000 manually annotated images, 8 discrete expressions). Relevant as a benchmark-scale reference point, and later (Section D/E below) as a dataset whose demographic composition has itself been questioned.
- **Savchenko, "HSEmotion: High-speed emotion recognition library," Software Impacts (2022), ScienceDirect / arXiv.** This is the actual baseline classifier already used throughout this thesis's own experiments. Confirmed real, EfficientNet-based, trained primarily on AffectNet-derived data. Directly relevant as the "Existing Response" already evaluated in this repository (`docs/RESEARCH_FINDINGS.md`), not as new literature evidence about the practical problem.

### B. Face Representation / ArcFace

- **Deng, Guo, Xue & Zafeiriou, "ArcFace: Additive Angular Margin Loss for Deep Face Recognition," CVPR 2019.** Confirmed via CVF Open Access and dblp. The original ArcFace paper — proposes an additive angular margin loss for face **identity verification**, evaluated on face-verification benchmarks (e.g., MegaFace). It is not an expression-recognition paper; it was never designed or evaluated for FER. This is the exact same model family (`w600k_r50`/`buffalo_l`) already used in this thesis's R5–R9 experiments as a frozen feature extractor.
- **Altaha, Jarraya, Hamdani & Alimi, "Facial Expression Recognition based on ArcFace Features and TinySiamese Network," 2023 International Conference on Cyberworlds (CW), pp. 24–31, IEEE.** Confirmed via IEEE Xplore listing and a corroborating ResearchGate/search-result record. **Directly uses ArcFace (ResNet-50 + ArcFace loss) as a feature extractor for FER**, paired with a "TinySiamese" network for classification, evaluated on FER2013, RAF-DB, and ExpW. This is a **direct precedent** for the "ArcFace features + downstream classifier for FER" design used in this thesis's R5–R7 experiments.
- **Waldner & Mitra, "Pairwise Discernment of AffectNet Expressions with ArcFace," arXiv:2412.01860 (2024).** Confirmed via direct fetch. Uses ArcFace (among other face-recognition backbones — ResNeXt, EfficientNet) via transfer learning for FER on AffectNet, specifically to address class imbalance through pairwise (two-emotion-at-a-time) classification rather than full multi-class classification. A **second direct precedent** for ArcFace-features-for-FER, and additionally relevant to this thesis's own rare-class problem (Angry=1, Disgust=2 ground truth).
- General transfer-learning-for-FER literature (MDPI "Transfer Learning for Facial Expression Recognition," and related work found in search but not individually fetched/verified beyond title-level — **flagged as requiring verification before citation in a final proposal**) indicates that using a face-recognition-pretrained backbone (not specifically ArcFace) as a frozen or partially-frozen feature extractor for FER is a broadly used strategy in the field, not a novel strategy in general.

### C. Skin Tone / Demographic Robustness

- **Buolamwini & Gebru, "Gender Shades: Intersectional Accuracy Disparities in Commercial Gender Classification," Proceedings of Machine Learning Research (PMLR) vol. 81, Conference on Fairness, Accountability and Transparency (FAT*) 2018.** Confirmed via PMLR proceedings page. Landmark paper: commercial *gender* classification (not expression) systems showed up to 34.7% error for darker-skinned females vs. 0.8% for lighter-skinned males; existing facial-analysis benchmark datasets were found to be 79.6–86.2% lighter-skinned. **This paper is about gender classification, not FER** — it is included here as the foundational methodological precedent for skin-tone-stratified accuracy auditing (the same style of analysis this thesis's own Fisher's-exact-test skin-tone comparison performs), not as direct FER evidence.
- **Fitzpatrick skin-type scale**: widely used in the fairness-audit literature following Buolamwini & Gebru (confirmed via multiple corroborating sources: Fitzpatrick17k dataset documentation, "Review of Demographic Bias in Face Recognition," arXiv:2502.02309). The primary Fitzpatrick (1988) dermatology source itself was **not independently fetched or verified in this review** — flagged as requiring verification before citation. Relevant caveat found in the literature and directly applicable to this thesis: **the Fitzpatrick scale has been explicitly critiqued for conflation with race/ethnicity**, and for overestimating certain skin types in some populations — this is a direct, literature-level reason to reinforce (not just internally assert) this thesis's existing rule that skin tone must not be treated as ethnicity.
- **"Racial Bias within Face Recognition: A Survey," ACM Computing Surveys (arXiv:2305.00817, confirmed listing found via search, full text not independently fetched in this pass — flagged as requiring verification for exact author list before final citation).** Survey-level evidence that skin-tone/racial accuracy disparities in face **recognition** (identity, not expression) are a well-documented, active research area.

### D. Error Analysis (Neutral/Angry confusion specifically)

- **Chhua, Wen, Hathalia, Zhu & O'Brien, "From Bias to Balance: Detecting Facial Expression Recognition Biases in Large Multimodal Foundation Models," arXiv:2408.14842 (2024).** Confirmed via direct fetch. Benchmarks four large multimodal models (GPT-4o, PaliGemma, Gemini, CLIP) on the RADIATE dataset and finds, specifically: **"Anger is misclassified as Disgust 2.1 times more often in Black Females than White Females"** (for GPT-4o). This is a **directly relevant, verified, demographic-stratified FER confusion-pattern finding** — though the specific confusion pair (Anger→Disgust) and the systems tested (large multimodal foundation models, not lightweight CNN-based classifiers like HSEmotion or an ArcFace+LR pipeline) differ from this thesis's own Neutral→Angry (HSEmotion) finding. It should **not** be cited as if it directly reproduces this thesis's specific error pattern — it establishes that *demographic-stratified, class-specific FER confusion patterns of this general kind* are a documented phenomenon in at least one other system/dataset, not that *this specific* pattern is established in general.
- **Hosseini, Pourramezan Fard & Mahoor, "Faces of Fairness: Examining Bias in Facial Expression Recognition Datasets and Models," arXiv:2502.11049.** Confirmed via direct fetch; **publication year metadata as retrieved was inconsistent (arXiv identifier numbering implies 2025, fetched page metadata read "2026") — flagged as requiring verification before citation in a final proposal.** Finds that four FER datasets (AffectNet, ExpW, FER2013, RAF-DB) all carry "significant demographic bias, most notably in race," with race the attribute showing the most pronounced imbalance, and that Transformer-based models (ViT, CLIP) show the largest fairness disparities among the models tested. Directly relevant to Framing B.

### E. Dataset / Benchmark Limitations

- **Li & Deng, "A Deeper Look at Facial Expression Dataset Bias," IEEE Transactions on Affective Computing (submitted 2019, published 2020).** Confirmed via direct fetch (arXiv:1904.11150). Investigates cross-dataset bias in FER caused by "different cultures and collection conditions," proposing a domain-adaptation method (ECAN) to address both marginal/conditional distribution mismatch and class imbalance across FER datasets. Establishes, from a different angle than the race/skin-tone papers above, that FER datasets are known in the literature to carry population/context-dependent biases that affect cross-dataset performance.
- **RAF-DB and FER2013 demographic composition**: per corroborating search results (not independently fetched at the primary-source level in this pass — flagged as requiring verification), RAF-DB's demographic composition is reported elsewhere as approximately 77% Caucasian, 8% African-American, 15% Asian, and FER2013/RAF-DB are both reported to show "White" as the plurality/majority demographic category across samples. **This is consistent in direction with (though not proof of) the general concern already raised in `docs/proposal/research/PROBLEM_ARCHITECTURE.md`** that FER benchmark data may not include dark-skinned populations at scale — but this thesis's own dataset (Pesta Babi film frames) is not RAF-DB, AffectNet, or FER2013, and no direct claim is made here that this thesis's dataset shares RAF-DB's or FER2013's specific demographic profile.

## 4. Existing Solutions

Answering directly: **what solutions already exist for the identified problem** (systematic FER misclassification patterns, and/or skin-tone-correlated accuracy variation)?

1. **General-purpose deep FER classifiers trained on large in-the-wild datasets** (e.g., HSEmotion/AffectNet-trained EfficientNet models — the actual baseline already used in this thesis). What it does: classifies expression directly from a face image. What problem it addresses: general expression classification at scale/speed. Evidence: widely used, published (Savchenko 2022), and this thesis's own R1–R9 results characterize its behavior on this specific dataset. Limitation remaining: this thesis's own evidence shows a specific Neutral→Angry error concentration and a skin-tone-correlated accuracy difference on this dataset; the literature (Section 2D) shows demographic-stratified FER confusion patterns are a documented phenomenon in at least one other system, but does not establish that HSEmotion specifically has been characterized this way elsewhere.
2. **Face-recognition-embedding-based FER (ArcFace-as-feature-extractor + separate classifier)** — Altaha et al. (2023), Waldner & Mitra (2024). What it does: repurposes an identity-oriented face representation for expression classification via a downstream classifier. What problem it addresses: leveraging strong, widely-available pretrained face representations for FER, and (in Waldner & Mitra's case) addressing class imbalance. Evidence: published, evaluated on standard FER benchmarks (FER2013, RAF-DB, ExpW, AffectNet). Limitation remaining: **neither paper evaluates this approach on a dark-skinned or skin-tone-stratified population** — this is the specific angle not yet covered by the existing solutions found in this review.
3. **Domain-adaptation methods for cross-dataset FER bias** (Li & Deng, 2020, ECAN). What it does: reduces distribution mismatch when transferring an FER model across datasets/cultures/collection conditions. What problem it addresses: cross-dataset generalization. Evidence: published, evaluated across multiple lab-controlled and real-world FER datasets. Limitation remaining: this is a cross-dataset transfer solution, not a within-dataset skin-tone-stratified robustness solution — it addresses a related but distinct problem framing than this thesis's evidence base.
4. **Fairness-auditing methodology itself** (Buolamwini & Gebru 2018; Hosseini et al. 2025) — not a solution to the FER accuracy problem, but an existing, precedented **method of investigation** (skin-tone/race-stratified accuracy auditing) that this thesis's own Fisher's-exact-test skin-tone comparison already resembles in structure, though at a much smaller sample size (this thesis: N=46 Dark / N=32 Medium-Dark, vs. Gender Shades' purpose-built, larger, balanced benchmark).

**No existing solution found in this review directly addresses the combination this thesis's evidence base points toward**: FER performance/error characterization, specifically for a skin-tone-stratified, real-world (non-benchmark, documentary-film-sourced), non-Western-benchmark-composition population. This is noted here as an observation from the literature found, not yet claimed as a validated "gap" — that determination is made formally in Section 6.

## 7. ArcFace Novelty Check

**Explicit finding: using ArcFace (or ArcFace-family embeddings) as a frozen/near-frozen feature extractor for facial expression recognition, followed by a separate classifier, has already been done and published** — at minimum by Altaha, Jarraya, Hamdani & Alimi (2023, ResNet-50+ArcFace features → TinySiamese classifier, evaluated on FER2013/RAF-DB/ExpW) and by Waldner & Mitra (2024, ArcFace among other backbones via transfer learning → pairwise classification on AffectNet). This directly confirms the Phase 1 audit's caution (`docs/proposal/validation/PROPOSAL_GUIDELINE_AUDIT.md`, "Novelty" row) and the guideline's explicit instruction not to assume ArcFace-for-FER is itself novel — **it is not, as a general technique.**

**What, if anything, could be novel about the proposed research context/design/evaluation**, based on what this review found (and did not find):

- **Context/population**: neither Altaha et al. nor Waldner & Mitra evaluate on a skin-tone-stratified or dark-skinned-specific population — this review did not find a source combining ArcFace-for-FER with a skin-tone-stratified evaluation. This is a candidate novelty angle (context, per the guideline's novelty-form taxonomy, §14.5), **not yet confirmed absent from the wider literature** — this review is targeted, not systematic (Section 1), so this must be phrased as "not found in this review," not "does not exist."
- **Dataset**: this thesis's dataset (real-world, documentary-film-sourced face crops, non-benchmark) differs in provenance from FER2013/RAF-DB/ExpW/AffectNet used in the found ArcFace-for-FER precedents — a candidate novelty angle at the dataset/context level, again not yet confirmed unique.
- **Evaluation design**: this thesis's already-executed statistical rigor (paired McNemar, group-aware bootstrap CIs, Fisher's exact on skin-tone subgroups) was not something this review specifically checked against the found ArcFace-for-FER precedents' own evaluation rigor — **not established either way**, flagged as an open question (see Section 5, G4).

## 8. Skin-Tone Gap Check

Explicitly separating four concepts that must not be collapsed into each other, per the task instruction:

- **Skin tone** (as used in this thesis): an image-derived, LAB-luminance-based bucket (`Dark`, `Medium-Dark`, `Unknown`) computed from face-crop pixels. It is a **measured visual property**, not a self-reported or externally-assigned identity category.
- **Race**: a socially constructed categorization system, typically self-reported or externally assigned in the demographic-bias literature reviewed above (e.g., RAF-DB's reported "Caucasian/African-American/Asian" composition, Hosseini et al.'s "race" bias-axis finding). This thesis's dataset has **no race field**.
- **Ethnicity**: a distinct, typically self-identified cultural/ancestral category (e.g., "Papuan"), also **not present** as a field in this thesis's dataset.
- **Demographic representation**: the general concept of how balanced/imbalanced a dataset is across any of the above categories — a dataset-composition property, not itself a measurement of any individual.

**Does the current thesis evidence align with, extend, contradict, or fail to connect meaningfully to existing literature?**

- **Aligns (in general phenomenon, not specific measurement)**: the literature (Buolamwini & Gebru 2018; Hosseini et al. 2025; the RAF-DB/FER2013 demographic-composition findings) consistently reports that facial-analysis system accuracy varies across skin-tone/race-stratified subgroups, and that standard FER benchmark datasets are demographically skewed. This thesis's own Fisher's-exact-test finding (OR=5.88, p=0.0027, HSEmotion accuracy, Dark vs. Medium-Dark, N=135) is **directionally consistent** with this general literature pattern — an accuracy difference across a skin-tone-adjacent measurement was found here too.
- **Does not extend the literature's causal or ethnicity-specific claims**: none of the literature reviewed establishes a *causal* mechanism linking skin tone to FER accuracy (the strongest sources, e.g. Hosseini et al., report bias/imbalance findings, not controlled causal experiments), and none of it licenses collapsing skin tone into race or ethnicity, or race/ethnicity into a specific named ethnic group (e.g., Papuan). This thesis's own Fisher's exact result **must be held to the same standard**: it is reported here, as throughout this repository, strictly as an **in-sample association**, not causal evidence, not a race-bias claim, and not a Papuan-specific claim.
- **Cannot yet be said to "extend" the literature's specific findings**: this thesis's measurement (image-derived LAB-luminance skin-tone bucket) is methodologically different from the literature's typical measurement (self-reported/externally-assigned race, or Fitzpatrick scale) — a claim that this thesis's finding "confirms" or "extends" the race-bias literature would overstate the connection between two different measurement systems. The most defensible statement is that both measurement systems, independently, have found an accuracy disparity along a skin-tone-adjacent axis — not that they measure the same thing.
- **Genuine gap candidate remaining**: this review did not find literature specifically evaluating an FER system's accuracy using an *image-derived skin-tone bucket* (as opposed to self-reported race or Fitzpatrick clinical scoring) on a real-world, non-benchmark, non-Western-composition dataset — this is the most defensible, narrowly-scoped gap candidate from this section (see G2 in Section 5), provided it is stated with the measurement-system caveat above intact.

## 10. Claim Strength

### Supported by literature (external evidence, not this thesis's own data)

- ArcFace was designed and evaluated for face **verification**, not expression recognition (Deng et al. 2019).
- ArcFace-family embeddings have already been used as FER feature extractors, evaluated on standard FER benchmarks, by at least two published works (Altaha et al. 2023; Waldner & Mitra 2024).
- Facial-analysis system accuracy has been repeatedly shown, in other systems and other datasets, to vary across skin-tone/race-stratified subgroups (Buolamwini & Gebru 2018; Hosseini et al. 2025).
- Class-specific, demographic-stratified FER confusion patterns (a different pair — Anger→Disgust — in a different system class — large multimodal foundation models) have been directly documented (Chhua et al. 2024).
- Standard FER benchmark datasets carry known cross-dataset/cultural bias (Li & Deng 2020) and, per multiple corroborating (not all independently primary-source-verified) reports, demographically skewed composition.
- The Fitzpatrick skin-type scale has been explicitly critiqued in the literature for conflation with race/ethnicity — external support for this thesis's own internal rule never to equate skin tone with ethnicity.

### Supported by current experiments (R1–R9, this thesis's own data)

- HSEmotion's Neutral→Angry misclassification pattern: 18/76 = 23.68%, reproduced identically across N=227 and N=135 populations.
- ArcFace+LR vs. HSEmotion accuracy on N=135: 40.74% vs. 36.30%, not statistically significant (McNemar p=0.4614).
- ArcFace+LR's Neutral errors are distributed (Happy=15, Sad=14, Fear=8, Surprise=6) rather than concentrated.
- Skin-tone-stratified HSEmotion accuracy difference on N=135 (Dark 45.65% n=46, Medium-Dark 12.5% n=32), Fisher's exact OR=5.88, p=0.0027.
- Angry (n=1) / Disgust (n=2) ground-truth scarcity across every population stage checked (227/144/138).

### Supported by both (literature and thesis evidence independently point the same direction)

- Skin-tone/demographic-stratified accuracy variation in facial-analysis systems is a real, literature-documented phenomenon **in general**, and this thesis's own dataset shows an association consistent in *direction* with that general phenomenon — **but the two are not the same measurement** (image-derived skin-tone bucket vs. self-reported race/Fitzpatrick scale), and this must not be blurred.
- Using face-recognition embeddings (ArcFace-family) for FER via a downstream classifier is a real, published, working approach **in general**, and this thesis's own R5–R9 work is a specific, evaluated instance of it on a novel-context population — but the *technique itself* is not novel (Section 7).

### Not yet supported (requires additional research)

- That this thesis's specific Neutral→Angry pattern (as opposed to class-specific confusion in general) recurs in other FER systems or datasets.
- That the skin-tone/accuracy association found in this thesis is causal, or reflects anything about ethnicity, race, or Papuan populations specifically.
- That ArcFace+LR is generally superior to HSEmotion (not statistically established, and this review found no literature claiming general ArcFace-for-FER superiority over EfficientNet/AffectNet-trained classifiers either).
- That a combination of ArcFace-for-FER and skin-tone-stratified, real-world (non-benchmark) evaluation is genuinely absent from the wider literature (this review is targeted, not systematic — "not found here" ≠ "does not exist," per the guideline's own explicit warning).
- A final, validated research gap statement — Section 9's four options are candidates, not conclusions; a final gap requires supervisor input and, per the guideline, is expected to be revisited as Phase 4 (RQ Options) proceeds.
