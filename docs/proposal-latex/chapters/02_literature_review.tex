\section{ArcFace and Face Recognition Representations}
\label{sec:lit-arcface}
% Source: docs/proposal/research/STATE_OF_ART_AND_GAP.md, Section 2B, Section 7 (ArcFace Novelty Check)
ArcFace \citep{deng2019arcface} proposes an additive angular margin loss for face \emph{verification}, evaluated on face-verification benchmarks; it was not designed or evaluated for expression recognition.

\textbf{Important — novelty status (must not be silently omitted or softened):} per the targeted literature review in \texttt{docs/proposal/research/STATE\_OF\_ART\_AND\_GAP.md} \S7, using ArcFace-family embeddings as a frozen feature extractor for FER, followed by a separate classifier, \textbf{has already been published} and is \textbf{not} treated as novel in this proposal.

\section{Skin Tone and Demographic Robustness}
\label{sec:lit-skin-tone}
% Source: docs/proposal/research/STATE_OF_ART_AND_GAP.md, Section 2C, Section 8
\citet{buolamwini2018gender} found that commercial gender-classification systems showed substantially higher error rates for darker-skinned female subjects than for lighter-skinned male subjects, and that existing facial-analysis benchmark datasets were predominantly lighter-skinned.

\textbf{Important — concept separation (must be preserved verbatim in any future draft):} skin tone, race, ethnicity, and demographic representation are four distinct concepts and must not be collapsed into one another (\texttt{docs/proposal/research/STATE\_OF\_ART\_AND\_GAP.md} \S8). This thesis's dataset contains no race or ethnicity field. Any skin-tone finding discussed in this proposal is an image-derived measurement, not a race or ethnicity classification, and any accuracy difference reported across skin-tone categories is an \emph{association}, not a causal or biological claim.
